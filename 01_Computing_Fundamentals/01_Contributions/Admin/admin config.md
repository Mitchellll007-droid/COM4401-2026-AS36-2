## Administrative Assistant Workstation

### Role Requirements

The Administrative Assistant mainly carries out everyday office tasks such as managing emails, creating documents, working with spreadsheets, accessing web-based systems, attending online meetings and handling basic customer records.

Although these tasks are not as demanding as CAD or 3D rendering, the workstation still needs to perform well when several applications are being used at the same time. For example, the employee could have multiple browser tabs open alongside email, a spreadsheet, documents and an online meeting.

Because of this, I have focused on good everyday performance, smooth multitasking, fast storage, reliability and value for money. I have also avoided adding expensive hardware where it would not give the employee a noticeable benefit. This keeps the workstation suitable for the role without unnecessarily using the full £950 budget.

### Overall Build Justification

Overall, I designed the workstation around the actual needs of the Administrative Assistant rather than simply choosing the most powerful components that would fit within the £950 budget.

The employee needs good everyday performance and needs to be able to move between emails, documents, spreadsheets, browser-based systems and online meetings without the computer becoming slow when multitasking. The i5-12400, 16GB of RAM and 1TB NVMe SSD provide a balanced level of performance for this workload, while the monitor, keyboard and mouse, webcam and headset make it a complete workstation rather than only focusing on the components inside the PC.

I also considered where extra performance would actually make a difference. For example, I decided against a dedicated graphics card because the employee is not carrying out CAD, 3D modelling or rendering. I also chose 16GB rather than 32GB of RAM because the extra memory would be difficult to justify for normal office applications. This means the budget is being spent on hardware that will actually benefit the employee rather than increasing specifications just because money is available.

The current workstation costs £833.08, leaving £116.92 of the £950 allowance before the Ethernet cable is added. For all three Administrative Assistant workstations, the current total is £2,499.24 compared with the £2,850 role allocation. This leaves £350.76 available for the remaining network hardware and also provides some room for possible price changes.

Overall, I think this is a responsible use of the company's hardware budget because the workstation provides the performance, storage, display and peripherals required for the role without being unnecessarily over-specified. It also keeps money available for the more demanding CAD and 3D Visualisation workstations, where higher-performance hardware will have a much greater impact on the employees' work.
